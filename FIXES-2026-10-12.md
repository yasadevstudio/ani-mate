# ANI-MATE 0.5.2 — franchises, season strips, story arcs

Reported: *"dragonball super does not show the beerus arc, you cant see the
season splits on earlier seasons, just the latest ones, a lot of issues."*

Six fixes. Three of the reported symptoms were one bug, and the headline one was
a regression introduced by v0.5.1.

Regression harness added at `tests/franchise-regression.py`: **6/10 before,
10/10 after, three consecutive clean runs.** Five of the ten cases are v0.5.1
fixes that had to survive, and did.

---

## 1. Searching "Dragon Ball Super" opened Dragon Ball Z — THE REPORTED BUG

`groupByFranchise()` sorted members by format then date ascending and opened
`members[0]`. **v0.5.1 made it oldest-first on purpose**, so Apothecary Diaries
would open S1 rather than S2. Correct for a three-season show. Wrong for a
franchise: `'dragon ball'` is a FRANCHISE_PATTERN prefix that unions forty years
into a single card, so a search for a 2026 release opened a 1989 series.

The card now opens the **highest `search_score`** member, which `rankResults`
already computed against the query. `members` stays chronological because it is
the expanded list and the strip. With no season named, season 1 still scores
highest, so v0.5.1's behaviour is preserved rather than reverted.

## 2. 27 of 40 entries inherited the 1986 original's identity — THE ROOT CAUSE

`findAniMatch()` pass 3 matched on a shared prefix with an 8-character floor.
`normName('Dragon Ball')` is `dragonball`, ten characters, and every title in
the franchise starts with it. A 2018 film came back as **TV, 1986-02-26**, with
the 1986 entry's cover and description.

The v0.5.1 season guard could not catch it: none of those titles carry a season
number, so `seasonNum()` returned 1 for all of them and they "agreed".

A prefix match now requires the **remainder** to be empty or a season marker.
`dragonball` → `dragonballsuperbroly` leaves `superbroly`, which is neither, so
it is rejected. 14 unit cases cover Z, GT, Super, Broly, Heroes and the four
v0.5.1 season forms.

## 3. The season strip was a franchise dump

Dragon Ball Super's strip rendered **39 chips** — series, films, ONAs and OVAs
together, with seven different entries all labelled "S1".

Series now form the spine; everything else sits behind a single **"+N more"**
toggle, and whatever is open is always on the spine whatever its format.

Labels changed too. The old fallback returned `'S1'` for any TV entry with no
season number, so Z, GT, Kai, Super and Daima were indistinguishable. A chip is
now labelled by what **distinguishes it from its siblings**: the shared prefix
across the spine is removed and the remainder is the label. Numbered seasons
still win first, so Apothecary Diaries stays `S1 | S2 | S3`.

    before   39 chips, seven of them reading "S1"
    after    S1 | Z | GT | Kai | Kai (2014) | Super | Daima | Super: Beerus | +31 more

## 4. "Just the latest ones"

`ensureFranchiseSiblings()` returned early when two siblings were already on
hand, and `franchiseSiblings()` reads from whatever the **current search**
returned. The strip settled for the two or three most popular entries, which
skew recent, and the earlier seasons were never fetched. The early return is
gone; the franchise is fetched once and cached.

## 5. franchise_id was not stable across searches

The same show came back as franchise **502** from one query and **223** from
another, because the id was the union-find root over whatever that query
happened to return. Anything persisted against it — favourites, history, the
sibling cache — went stale.

Franchises matching a FRANCHISE_PATTERN now get a stable `fp:<pattern>` key.
Longest pattern wins, so `jojo's bizarre adventure` beats `jojo`.

Two further repairs fell out of this:
- **Franchise membership no longer requires an AniList match.** Tightening the
  prefix match was right, but it left films with no match and therefore no
  franchise — Super Hero dropped out of Dragon Ball entirely. Membership is a
  property of the title.
- **Unmatched entries are enriched by a targeted lookup.** `getAniListCovers()`
  was already making a batched aliased AniList request for covers; it now also
  asks for id, format, start date and popularity, so an entry AniList's paged
  search missed still gets its identity. No extra round trip. Cap raised 15 → 25.

## 6. Story arcs

Neither AllAnime nor AniList exposes arcs, so `/episodes` returned 131 flat
numbers and there was no way to find the Beerus saga.

`server/arcs.json` is the master, keyed by AniList id, validated on load and at
build time: ranges must be contiguous from 1 and end at the episode count, or
the entry is ignored. A wrong boundary is worse than none.
`scripts/build-arcs.js` generates `mobile/www/js/arcs.js` from it, so mobile —
which talks to AllAnime directly and never sees our server — does not carry a
second hand-maintained copy.

**The arcs ARE the range buttons.** A long series already chunked into
`1-50 | 51-100`; where arc data exists those bands become the arcs instead. No
second control, no new CSS.

    Dragon Ball Super
    God of Destruction Beerus 1-18 | Golden Frieza 19-27 |
    Universe 6 & Duplicate Vegeta 28-46 | Zamasu 47-76 | Universe Survival 77-131

Sourced from **Toei Animation's own promotional arcs** via Wikipedia's episode
list. An aggregator offered seven finer arcs with different boundaries (Beerus
1-15, a separate Copy-Vegeta, a Paradise Plant); those are fan subdivisions. The
rule is recorded in the file: prefer the publisher, note the disagreement.

## A NOTE ON THE TEST HARNESS

Its first version reported three passes that were not passes. An upstream hiccup
returned zero rows and `max([...] or [0]) <= 6` is true of nothing, while
`next((...), {}).get('format')` is `None` for a row that is not there. **Absent
data passed as success.** It now retries, asserts a minimum row count per case,
and raises rather than returning on empty.

It also failed a working fix once, because it still ported the old `members[0]`
card sort after the code had moved to score-based selection. A harness has to be
kept in step with the thing it measures, or it reports the wrong answer in both
directions.
